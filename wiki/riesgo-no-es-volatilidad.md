# El riesgo no es la volatilidad

Buffett define el riesgo con el diccionario, no con la estadística.

> "we define risk, using dictionary terms, as 'the possibility of loss or injury.'" — 1993

Rechaza la equivalencia académica entre riesgo y beta (volatilidad), que produce absurdos:
según ella, una acción que ha caído mucho es "más arriesgada" a precio bajo que a precio alto.

> "under beta-based theory, a stock that has dropped very sharply compared to the market - as
> had Washington Post when we bought it in 1973 - becomes 'riskier' at the lower price than it
> was at the higher price." — 1993

El riesgo real es de poder adquisitivo: que lo que recibas después de impuestos no conserve,
al menos, el poder de compra inicial más un interés modesto.

> "the real risk that an investor must assess is whether his aggregate after-tax receipts from
> an investment (including those he receives on sale) will, over his prospective holding
> period, give him at least as much purchasing power as he had to begin with, plus a modest
> rate of interest on that initial stake." — 1993

Para evaluarlo propone cinco factores, imprecisos pero útiles ("lo reconoces cuando lo ves"):

> "1) The certainty with which the long-term economic characteristics of the business can be
> evaluated; 2) The certainty with which management can be evaluated, both as to its ability to
> realize the full potential of the business and to wisely employ its cash flows; 3) The
> certainty with which management can be counted on to channel the rewards from the business to
> the shareholders rather than to itself; 4) The purchase price of the business; 5) The levels
> of taxation and inflation that will be experienced..." — 1993
