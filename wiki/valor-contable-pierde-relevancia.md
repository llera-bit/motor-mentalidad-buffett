# El valor contable pierde relevancia

El valor contable registra lo que se metió; el valor intrínseco estima lo que puede sacarse. En
1994 Buffett lleva la distinción a un ejemplo que no es una empresa: una carrera universitaria.

> "To see how historical input (book value) and future output (intrinsic value) can diverge,
> let's look at another form of investment, a college education. Think of the education's cost as
> its 'book value.'" — 1994

Dos alumnos pueden pagar lo mismo y obtener valores futuros incomparables. La conclusión es
tajante.

> "Some graduates will find that the book value of their education exceeds its intrinsic value,
> which means that whoever paid for the education didn't get his money's worth. In other cases,
> the intrinsic value of an education will far exceed its book value, a result that proves capital
> was wisely deployed. In all cases, what is clear is that book value is meaningless as an
> indicator of intrinsic value." — 1994

## La excepción que Berkshire fue durante décadas

Pese a esa sentencia general, Buffett usó durante casi treinta años el valor contable por acción
como aproximación al valor intrínseco de Berkshire, y explicaba por qué en su caso concreto sí
servía: la brecha entre ambos existía, pero era estable.

> "We have no way to pinpoint intrinsic value. But we do have a useful, though considerably
> understated, proxy for it: per-share book value. This yardstick is meaningless at most
> companies. At Berkshire, however, book value very roughly tracks business values." — 2011

## El abandono de la métrica

En 2018 retira el valor contable de la primera página de la carta y enumera las tres
circunstancias que lo dejaron obsoleto: Berkshire pasó de ser cartera a ser empresa operativa; las
filiales figuran en libros muy por debajo de su valor mientras las acciones cotizan a mercado; y
las recompras suben el valor intrínseco por acción mientras bajan el contable.

> "The fact is that the annual change in Berkshire's book value - which makes its farewell
> appearance on page 2 - is a metric that has lost the relevance it once had." — 2018

> "while our equity holdings are valued at market prices, accounting rules require our collection
> of operating companies to be included in book value at an amount far below their current value,
> a mismark that has grown in recent years." — 2018

> "Each transaction makes per-share intrinsic value go up, while per-share book value goes down.
> That combination causes the book-value scorecard to become increasingly out of touch with
> economic reality." — 2018

No es que la métrica se rompiera: es que el negocio que medía cambió de naturaleza. La regla de
1994 nunca dejó de valer; Berkshire simplemente dejó de ser su excepción.
