# Clayton Homes y la originación hipotecaria

En la carta de 2008 Buffett usa Clayton —el mayor fabricante de casas prefabricadas de EE. UU.—
para explicar la crisis desde dentro del negocio de prestar. Su tesis: el sector de la casa
prefabricada ya había vivido exactamente lo mismo entre 1997 y 2000, y nadie tomó nota.

> "This 1997-2000 fiasco should have served as a canary-in-the-coal-mine warning for the far-larger
> conventional housing market. But investors, government and rating agencies learned exactly nothing
> from the manufactured-home debacle." — 2008

## Qué falló

Se dejó de exigir entrada, y el incentivo del comisionista hizo el resto.

> "To begin with, the need for meaningful down payments was frequently ignored. Sometimes fakery was
> involved. ('That certainly looks like a $2,000 cat to me' says the salesman who will receive a
> $3,000 commission if the loan goes through.)" — 2008

Los préstamos se empaquetaban y se vendían, de modo que quien los concedía no soportaba su
consecuencia.

> "The resulting mortgages were usually packaged ('securitized') and sold by Wall Street firms to
> unsuspecting investors. This chain of folly had to end badly, and it did." — 2008

Clayton no lo hizo, y el dato lo respalda.

> "Clayton, it should be emphasized, followed far more sensible practices in its own lending
> throughout that time. Indeed, no purchaser of the mortgages it originated and then securitized has
> ever lost a dime of principal or interest." — 2008

## Lending 101

Los prestatarios de Clayton tienen rentas modestas y puntuaciones de crédito mediocres, y aun así
pagan. La explicación no tiene nada de sofisticada.

> "The answer is elementary, going right back to Lending 101. Our borrowers simply looked at how
> full-bore mortgage payments would compare with their actual - not hoped-for - income and then
> decided whether they could live with that commitment. Simply put, they took out a mortgage with the
> intention of paying it off, whatever the course of home prices." — 2008

Y lo que **no** hicieron importa tanto como lo que hicieron.

> "They did not count on making their loan payments by means of refinancing. They did not sign up for
> 'teaser' rates that upon reset were outsized relative to their income. And they did not assume that
> they could always sell their home at a profit if their mortgage payments became onerous. Jimmy
> Stewart would have loved these folks." — 2008

La conclusión es que un préstamo se juzga por la capacidad de pago del deudor, no por el valor
esperado del colateral.
