# Criterios de selección (cuatro filtros)

Ya en su primera carta (1977) Buffett fija el marco con el que selecciona acciones
cotizadas: exactamente igual que evaluaría la compra de un negocio entero. De ahí salen
cuatro filtros explícitos.

> "We select our marketable equity securities in much the same way we would evaluate a
> business for acquisition in its entirety. We want the business to be (1) one that we
> can understand, (2) with favorable long-term prospects, (3) operated by honest and
> competent people, and (4) available at a very attractive price." — 1977

Los cuatro filtros:
1. Un negocio **que podamos entender**.
2. Con **perspectivas favorables a largo plazo**.
3. Dirigido por **gente honesta y competente**.
4. Disponible a un **precio muy atractivo**.

El corolario sobre el precio: no compra buscando un buen comportamiento de la cotización
a corto plazo; y si el negocio sigue satisfaciendo, da la **bienvenida a las caídas** de
precio como oportunidad para comprar más barato.

> "We ordinarily make no attempt to buy equities for anticipated favorable stock price
> behavior in the short term. In fact, if their business experience continues to satisfy
> us, we welcome lower market prices of stocks we own as an opportunity to acquire even
> more of a good thing at a better price." — 1977

De dónde vienen esas gangas: porciones proporcionales de negocios excelentes a veces se
venden en bolsa con grandes descuentos frente a lo que costarían en una transacción
negociada por la empresa entera.

> "bargains in business ownership, which simply are not available directly through
> corporate acquisition, can be obtained indirectly through stock ownership." — 1977

En 1978 repite los cuatro filtros casi palabra por palabra, y precisa que es el filtro (4),
el precio, el que suele impedir actuar:

> "We get excited enough to commit a big percentage of insurance company net worth to
> equities only when we find (1) businesses we can understand, (2) with favorable long-term
> prospects, (3) operated by honest and competent people, and (4) priced very
> attractively." — 1978

> "We usually can identify a small number of potential investments meeting requirements
> (1), (2) and (3), but (4) often prevents action." — 1978

Como comprador neto en la mayoría de los años, prefiere incluso que esos precios no suban:

> "We are not concerned with whether the market quickly revalues upward securities that we
> believe are selling at bargain prices. In fact, we prefer just the opposite since, in
> most years, we expect to have funds available to be a net buyer of securities." — 1978
