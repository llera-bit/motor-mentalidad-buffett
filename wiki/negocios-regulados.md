# Negocios regulados e intensivos en capital

BNSF (ferrocarril) y MidAmerican Energy (utilities) son tan distintos del resto de Berkshire que
Buffett les da su propia sección en la carta y los separa en el balance.

> "We have two very large businesses, BNSF and MidAmerican Energy, that have important common
> characteristics distinguishing them from our many other businesses. Consequently, we assign them
> their own sector in this letter and also split out their combined financial statistics in our GAAP
> balance sheet and income statement." — 2011

## Qué los distingue

Activos de vida larguísima, regulados, financiados con deuda propia que Berkshire no garantiza.

> "A key characteristic of both companies is the huge investment they have in very long-lived,
> regulated assets, with these funded by large amounts of long-term debt that is not guaranteed by
> Berkshire. Our credit is not needed: Both businesses have earning power that, even under very
> adverse business conditions, amply covers their interest requirements." — 2010

> "Both businesses have earning power that even under terrible business conditions amply covers their
> interest requirements." — 2011

Son lo contrario de [[sees-candies]]: no crecen sin capital, viven de tragarlo. Y aun así Buffett
los compra, porque el regulador garantiza un retorno razonable sobre ese capital.

## El pacto social

La relación con el regulador no es un impuesto: es la contrapartida de invertir eternamente.

> "Both companies are heavily regulated, and both will have a never-ending need to make major
> investments in plant and equipment. Both also need to provide efficient, customer-satisfying
> service to earn the respect of their communities and regulators. In return, both need to be assured
> that they will be allowed to earn reasonable earnings on future capital investments." — 2010

> "Wise regulation and wise investment are two sides of the same coin." — 2010

> "At MidAmerican, we participate in a similar 'social compact.' We are expected to put up
> ever-increasing sums to satisfy the future needs of our customers." — 2010

Es el "buen negocio" de la tríada de 2007: retorno aceptable, no extraordinario, sobre cantidades
enormes de capital incremental. Buffett lo compra precisamente porque Berkshire tiene más efectivo
del que sus grandes negocios pueden absorber.
