# La locura de las adquisiciones

Buffett diagnostica en 1981 por qué tantos directivos pagan primas altísimas por comprar
empresas. Sospecha tres motivaciones, casi siempre tácitas.

Primera, los espíritus animales: a los líderes les sobra energía y les atrae la actividad.

> "Leaders, business or otherwise, seldom are deficient in animal spirits and often relish
> increased activity and challenge. At Berkshire, the corporate pulse never beats faster
> than when an acquisition is in prospect." — 1981

Segunda, el culto al tamaño por encima de la rentabilidad.

> "Most organizations, business or otherwise, measure themselves, are measured by others,
> and compensate their managers far more by the yardstick of size than by any other
> yardstick." — 1981

Tercera, el exceso de optimismo: la certeza de que el "beso" del gestor transformará al
sapo en príncipe.

> "Many managements apparently were overexposed in impressionable childhood years to the
> story in which the imprisoned handsome prince is released from a toad's body by a kiss
> from a beautiful princess. Consequently, they are certain their managerial kiss will do
> wonders for the profitability of Company T(arget)." — 1981

Su conclusión: el inversor ya puede comprar sapos al precio de sapos; pagar el doble por el
derecho a besarlos rara vez obra el milagro.

> "investors can always buy toads at the going price for toads. If investors instead
> bankroll princesses who wish to pay double for the right to kiss the toad, those kisses
> had better pack some real dynamite. We've observed many kisses but very few miracles." — 1981

En 1982, viendo las grandes adquisiciones ajenas, describe cómo la adrenalina vence al
intelecto directivo.

> "The thrill of the chase blinded the pursuers to the consequences of the catch." — 1982

Y recurre a Pascal para nombrar el fondo del problema.

> "It has struck me that all men's misfortunes spring from the single cause that they are
> unable to stay quietly in one room." — 1982 (Buffett citando a Pascal)
