# Hiperactividad del mercado

Buffett describe el coste de la rotación excesiva: la fricción de comprar y vender es un
impuesto que los inversores se imponen a sí mismos, aunque no lo llamen así.

> "Can you imagine the agonized cry that would arise if a governmental unit were to impose a
> new 16 2/3% tax on earnings of corporations or investors? By market activity, investors
> can impose upon themselves the equivalent of such a tax." — 1983

De ahí su sentencia sobre la actividad frenética.

> "A hyperactive stock market is the pickpocket of enterprise." — 1983

Lo que beneficia al intermediario no beneficia al cliente.

> "what is good for the croupier is not good for the customer." — 1983

Y una regla sobre quién produce esa volatilidad: quien compra sin mirar el valor, vende sin
mirarlo.

> "People who buy for non-value reasons are likely to sell for non-value reasons." — 1983

En 1986 lo cuantifica: por los costes de transacción y gestión, el conjunto de los
accionistas está condenado a rendir menos que las empresas que posee.

> "because of the heavy transaction and investment management costs they bear, stockholders
> as a whole and over the long term must inevitably underperform the companies they own... Bull
> markets can obscure mathematical laws, but they cannot repeal them." — 1986

Y define al gestor hiperactivo con sarcasmo.

> "the term 'institutional investor' is becoming one of those self-contradictions called an
> oxymoron, comparable to 'jumbo shrimp,' 'lady mudwrestler' and 'inexpensive lawyer.'" — 1986

En 2004 lo sintetiza para el inversor medio: sus enemigos son la emoción y los costes (por
operar en exceso, seguir modas y entrar/salir a destiempo).

> "Investors should remember that excitement and expenses are their enemies." — 2004
