# Owner earnings

En el apéndice de 1986 (a propósito de Scott Fetzer) Buffett define los "owner earnings" —el
beneficio real para el dueño— como el beneficio contable más las cargas no monetarias, menos
la inversión de mantenimiento que el negocio necesita para conservar su posición.

> "These represent (a) reported earnings plus (b) depreciation, depletion, amortization, and
> certain other non-cash charges... less (c) the average annual amount of capitalized
> expenditures for plant and equipment, etc. that the business requires to fully maintain its
> long-term competitive position and its unit volume." — 1986

Prefiere esta cifra, aunque sea imprecisa, a la del GAAP para valorar.

> "we consider the owner earnings figure, not the GAAP figure, to be the relevant item for
> valuation purposes - both for investors in buying stocks and for managers in buying entire
> businesses. We agree with Keynes's observation: 'I would rather be vaguely right than
> precisely wrong.'" — 1986

De ahí su crítica a la falacia del "cash flow": sumar (a) + (b) sin restar (c).

> "you shouldn't add (b) without subtracting (c)... The company or investor believing that
> the debt-servicing ability or the equity valuation of an enterprise can be measured by
> totaling (a) and (b) while ignoring (c) is headed for certain trouble." — 1986

Y su recordatorio sobre la contabilidad:

> "accounting is but an aid to business thinking, never a substitute for it." — 1986

En 1989 lo aplica contra la métrica EBITDA: ignorar la depreciación es delirante, porque en
casi todos los negocios el capex que iguala a la depreciación es un gasto tan real como los
salarios.

> "At 95% of American businesses, capital expenditures that over time roughly approximate
> depreciation are a necessity and are every bit as real an expense as labor or utility costs.
> Even a high school dropout knows that to finance a car he must have income that covers not
> only interest and operating expenses, but also realistically-calculated depreciation. He
> would be laughed out of the bank if he started talking about EBDIT." — 1989
