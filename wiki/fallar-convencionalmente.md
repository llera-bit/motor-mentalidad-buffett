# Fallar convencionalmente

Buffett describe el incentivo perverso que empuja a los gestores a la decisión convencional
aunque sea peor: la asimetría entre premio y castigo personal.

> "Most managers have very little incentive to make the intelligent-but-with-some-chance-of-
> looking-like-an-idiot decision. Their personal gain/loss ratio is all too obvious: if an
> unconventional decision works out well, they get a pat on the back and, if it works out
> poorly, they get a pink slip." — 1984

De ahí la regla tácita: es más seguro fracasar con la manada.

> "Failing conventionally is the route to go; as a group, lemmings may have a rotten image,
> but no individual lemming has ever received bad press." — 1984

Buffett y Munger escapan de ese incentivo porque actúan como dueños, no como gestores a
sueldo.

> "With 47% of Berkshire's stock, Charlie and I don't worry about being fired, and we
> receive our rewards as owners, not managers. Thus we behave with Berkshire's money as we
> would with our own. That frequently leads us to unconventional behavior." — 1984
