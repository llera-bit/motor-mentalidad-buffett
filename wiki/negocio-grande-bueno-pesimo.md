# El gran negocio, el bueno y el pésimo

En 2007 Buffett clasifica los negocios por la economía del capital que necesitan para crecer.
Lo que distingue a un gran negocio no es solo su foso, sino que produce retornos enormes sin
exigir apenas capital incremental.

> "Long-term competitive advantage in a stable industry is what we seek in a business. If that
> comes with rapid organic growth, great. But even without organic growth, such a business is
> rewarding... Truly great businesses, earning huge returns on tangible assets, can't for any
> extended period reinvest a large portion of their earnings internally at high rates of
> return." — 2007

El **gran** negocio es See's: comprado por 25 M en 1972, solo ha necesitado reinvertir 32 M desde
entonces, y ha entregado 1.350 M de beneficio antes de impuestos.

> "It's far better to have an ever-increasing stream of earnings with virtually no major capital
> requirements. Ask Microsoft or Google." — 2007

El **bueno** tiene ventaja duradera pero debe reinvertir mucho para crecer (FlightSafety, las
utilities): "poner más para ganar más", con retornos aceptables sobre ese capital incremental,
no extraordinarios.

El **pésimo** crece rápido, devora capital y apenas gana dinero: las aerolíneas.

> "The worst sort of business is one that grows rapidly, requires significant capital to engender
> the growth, and then earns little or no money. Think airlines... Investors have poured money
> into a bottomless pit, attracted by growth when they should have been repelled by it." — 2007

Su imagen para recordarlo: tres cuentas de ahorro.

> "The great one pays an extraordinarily high interest rate that will rise as the years pass. The
> good one pays an attractive rate of interest that will be earned also on deposits that are
> added. Finally, the gruesome account both pays an inadequate interest rate and requires you to
> keep adding money at those disappointing returns." — 2007

Corolario de asignación de capital: no hay obligación de reinvertir donde se ganó el dinero;
muchas veces es un error hacerlo.

> "There's no rule that you have to invest money where you've earned it. Indeed, it's often a
> mistake to do so." — 2007
