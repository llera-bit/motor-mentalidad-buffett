# El negocio textil

Berkshire Hathaway *era* una textil. En julio de 1985 Buffett la cerró y dedicó a la autopsia una
de las secciones más largas y honestas de todas sus cartas.

> "In July we decided to close our textile operation, and by yearend this unpleasant job was largely
> completed. The history of this business is instructive." — 1985

## Un negocio de materia prima

> "The domestic textile industry operates in a commodity business, competing in a world market in
> which substantial excess capacity exists." — 1985

Los gestores fueron excelentes y los trabajadores no tuvieron culpa: cobraban poco y el sindicato,
consciente de la posición de costes, nunca apretó. Buffett saca de ahí una conclusión amarga.

> "Ironically, we would have been better off financially if our union had behaved unreasonably some
> years ago; we then would have recognized the impossible future that we faced, promptly closed down,
> and avoided significant future losses." — 1985

Y se acusa a sí mismo de haber creído lo que quería creer.

> "I ignored Comte's advice - 'the intellect should be the servant of the heart, but not its slave' -
> and believed what I preferred to believe." — 1985

## La trampa del capex que parece rentable

Cada inversión propuesta pasaba el test del retorno sobre la inversión —y prometía más que gastar lo
mismo en See's o en el periódico. Y sin embargo todas eran ilusorias.

> "Each proposal to do so looked like an immediate winner. Measured by standard return-on-investment
> tests, in fact, these proposals usually promised greater economic benefits than would have resulted
> from comparable expenditures in our highly-profitable candy and newspaper businesses." — 1985

> "But the promised benefits from these textile investments were illusory. Many of our competitors,
> both domestic and foreign, were stepping up to the same kind of expenditures and, once enough
> companies did so, their reduced costs became the baseline for reduced prices industrywide." — 1985

> "After each round of investment, all the players had more money in the game and returns remained
> anemic." — 1985

## El contrafactual: Burlington

Burlington era veinte veces mayor y decidió quedarse. Invirtió 3.000 M en veintiún años. El
resultado, medido en poder adquisitivo, fue destrucción de valor.

> "In 1964 Burlington had sales of $1.2 billion against our $50 million... Its stock sold at 60 at
> the end of 1964; ours was 13." — 1985

> "Nevertheless, Burlington has lost sales volume in real dollars and has far lower returns on sales
> and equity now than 20 years ago... each share commands about one-third the purchasing power it did
> at the end of 1964." — 1985

Y la moraleja de la asignación de capital, que Buffett generaliza: no es que Burlington decidiera
mal, es que decidió racionalmente dentro de un negocio malo.

> "Given Burlington's basic commitment to stay in textiles, I would also surmise that the company's
> capital decisions were quite rational." — 1985

## Dónde se para: la frontera moral

Buffett rechaza cerrar un negocio rentable solo para ganar un poco más, y rechaza financiar
indefinidamente uno que solo promete pérdidas. Sitúa su posición entre los dos extremos, con nombre
y apellidos.

> "I also feel it inappropriate for even an exceptionally profitable company to fund an operation
> once it appears to have unending losses in prospect. Adam Smith would disagree with my first
> proposition, and Karl Marx would disagree with my second; the middle ground is the only position
> that leaves me comfortable." — 1985
