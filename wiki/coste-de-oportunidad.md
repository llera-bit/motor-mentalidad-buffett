# Coste de oportunidad

Toda decisión de capital se juzga contra la mejor alternativa disponible, no contra cero. Buffett
compara cada compra con todas las demás opciones abiertas —incluida la de comprar un trozo del
mejor negocio del mundo en bolsa—, una disciplina que, dice, casi ningún gestor practica.

> "we always mentally compare any move we are contemplating with dozens of other opportunities
> open to us, including the purchase of small pieces of the best businesses in the world via the
> stock market. Our practice of making this comparison - acquisitions against passive investments
> - is a discipline that managers focused simply on expansion seldom use." — 1995

De ahí el corolario que rompe la costumbre corporativa: no hay ninguna obligación de reinvertir
el dinero donde se ganó.

> "There's no rule that you have to invest money where you've earned it. Indeed, it's often a
> mistake to do so." — 2007

En 2010 nombra la trampa: la mayoría de las empresas se autolimitan a reinvertir dentro de su
propio sector, un universo diminuto y a menudo malo. Berkshire no tiene esa atadura.

> "Most companies limit themselves to reinvesting funds within the industry in which they have
> been operating. That often restricts them, however, to a 'universe' for capital allocation that
> is both tiny and quite inferior to what is available in the wider world." — 2010

> "we also measure businesses against opportunities available in marketable securities, a
> comparison most managements don't make. Often, businesses are priced ridiculously high against
> what can likely be earned from investments in stocks or bonds. At such moments, we buy
> securities and bide our time." — 2010

Y confiesa haber caído él mismo en la trampa durante años, con la fábrica textil.

> "The dumbest thing I could have done was to pursue 'opportunities' to improve and expand the
> existing textile operation - so for years that's exactly what I did." — 2010
