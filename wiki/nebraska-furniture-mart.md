# Nebraska Furniture Mart

En 1983 Buffett compra la mayoría de Nebraska Furniture Mart y presenta a Rose Blumkin. Es el caso
en el que la ventaja competitiva y el gestor son la misma persona.

> "the high point of 1983 - the acquisition of a majority interest in Nebraska Furniture Mart and our
> association with Rose Blumkin and her family." — 1983

El año anterior había citado a Pascal —la desgracia de los hombres nace de no saber quedarse quietos
en una habitación— para explicar las adquisiciones tontas ([[locura-de-las-adquisiciones]]). Ahora
hace la única excepción.

> "Even Pascal would have left the room for Mrs. Blumkin." — 1983

## La biografía como dato de negocio

> "About 67 years ago Mrs. Blumkin, then 23, talked her way past a border guard to leave Russia for
> America. She had no formal education, not even at the grammar school level, and knew no
> English." — 1983

> "In 1937, after many years of selling used clothing, Mrs. Blumkin had saved $500 with which to
> realize her dream of opening a furniture store." — 1983

## El resultado

Un solo establecimiento que factura más que ningún otro del país.

> "Today Nebraska Furniture Mart generates over $100 million of sales annually out of one 200,000
> square-foot store. No other home furnishings store in the country comes close to that volume." — 1983
