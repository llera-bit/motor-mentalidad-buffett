# Incentivos alineados

Buffett critica el uso indiscriminado de las opciones a precio fijo a diez años: premian la
mera retención de beneficios, no la habilidad con el capital.

> "Many stock options in the corporate world have worked in exactly that fashion: they have
> gained in value simply because management retained earnings, not because it did well with
> the capital in its hands." — 1985

Y son ciegas al desempeño individual: el holgazán cobra igual que la estrella.

> "Once granted, the option is blind to individual performance... the sluggard receives
> rewards from his options precisely as does the star. A managerial Rip Van Winkle, ready to
> doze for ten years, could not wish for a better 'incentive' system." — 1985

Su alternativa: atar la recompensa a lo que cada uno controla, como en el béisbol.

> "Managers with limited areas of responsibility should have incentives that pay off in
> relation to results under their control. The .350 hitter expects, and also deserves, a big
> payoff for his performance - even if he plays for a cellar-dwelling team. And the .150
> hitter should get no reward - even if he plays for a pennant winner." — 1985

El alineamiento real llega cuando el gestor compra acciones y asume su riesgo y su coste.

> "By accepting both the risks and the carrying costs that go with outright purchases, these
> managers truly walk in the shoes of owners." — 1985
