# Círculo de competencia

Buffett atribuye su éxito no a resolver problemas difíciles, sino a evitarlos: buscar
obstáculos de un palmo que se pueden pasar por encima, en vez de intentar saltar los de dos
metros.

> "we concentrated on identifying one-foot hurdles that we could step over rather than because
> we acquired any ability to clear seven-footers." — 1989

> "in both business and investments it is usually far more profitable to simply stick with the
> easy and obvious than it is to resolve the difficult... we've done better by avoiding dragons
> than by slaying them." — 1989

Quedarse dentro del círculo tiene un coste asumible: perder oportunidades fuera de él no es
pecado.

> "It's no sin to miss a great opportunity outside one's area of competence." — 1989

Y lo distingue de tener una opinión negativa: sobre los negocios que no entiende, no
pronostica; se declara agnóstico, no ateo.

> "we have no ability to forecast the economics of the investment banking business... the
> airline industry, or the paper industry. This does not mean that we predict a negative
> future for these industries: we're agnostics, not atheists." — 1989

En 1990 lo ilustra con los gestores de Wells Fargo, que se ciñen a lo que entienden, y con la
máxima de Thomas Watson.

> "both stick with what they understand and let their abilities, not their egos, determine
> what they attempt. (Thomas J. Watson Sr. of IBM followed the same rule: 'I'm no genius,' he
> said. 'I'm smart in spots - but I stay around those spots.')" — 1990
